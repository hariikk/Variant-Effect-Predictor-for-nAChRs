Survey Summary: Twenty-Two Methods for Direction-Aware Variant Eect Prediction in nAChRs
Hari Krishna Mohan Raj
August 30, 2026
Abstract
This note summarises the twenty-two papers retained in the project survey sheet. It groups them by the technology each one contributes, describes the problem each is targeting, and maps each paper onto the specic goal of this project: predicting gain-of-function versus lossof-function direction for missense variants in nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs).
1 Overview
The twenty-two papers fall into eight technological clusters, summarised in Table 1. Six of them predict direction of eect directly and are the closest comparators to our model; the rest supply either the feature representations (protein language models, structure prediction, stability estimation), the benchmark infrastructure, or the structural-biology data (cryo-EM nAChR structures) that our pipeline builds on.
2 Technologies in use
Protein language models (dominant). Seven of the twenty-two papers are built on transformer
language models trained on protein sequence alone. Rives et al. established the paradigm with ESM1b; Meier et al. showed a masked model can score mutations zero-shot as a log-likelihood ratio; and Lin et al. repurposed the same representation for structure prediction (ESMFold). Marquet et al. and Brandes et al. both demonstrate that embeddings are useful, cheap features, the former for conservation and the latter as the input to a logistic-regression pathogenicity head. Two papers push scale in opposite directions: Chen et al. train a 100-billion-parameter model (xTrimoPGLM), while Sledzieski et al. use parameter-ecient ne-tuning (LoRA), which matters most to us because our dataset is tiny.
Generative and evolutionary models. Frazer et al. (EVE) train a Bayesian variational au-
toencoder across multiple sequence alignments to score pathogenicity from evolution alone. It is the strongest pure-conservation signal in the survey but requires deep MSAs and, like all pathogenicity models, does not distinguish GOF from LOF.
Structure prediction. AlphaFold2 (Jumper et al.) and AlphaFold3 (Abramson et al.) supply
atomic coordinates: AlphaFold2 for single-chain structure at experimental accuracy, and AlphaFold3 for complexes that include ligands and ions. Both are directly relevant because our structural features are extracted from experimental cryo-EM maps and AlphaFold models.

over sequence and structure, and Blaabjerg et al. (RaSP) use a graph neural network over ESM-1v embeddings to predict stability change (G). Both show that structure-aware deep networks extract signal that sequence-only models miss, which motivates the structural branch of our feature set.
Direction-of-eect predictors. Six papers target the exact problem this project addresses. fun-
NCion (Brunklaus et al.) classies GOF versus LOF for sodium and calcium channel variants; two related works by Boÿelmann et al. apply multi-task and multi-kernel support-vector machines to voltage-gated potassium, sodium, and calcium channels, the latter adding clinical phenotype (HPO) features; LoGoFunc (Stein et al.) predicts GOF/LOF genome-wide with a gradient-boosted ensemble; PreMode (Zhong et al.) uses an SE(3)-equivariant graph neural network; and MissION (Gies et al.) combines ESM-2 embeddings with structural, residue, and ontology features in separate channels to reach ROC-AUC 0.925.
Pathogenicity comparator. AlphaMissense (Cheng et al.) ne-tunes AlphaFold2 on population-
frequency data to classify the clinical signicance of every human missense variant. It is the key pathogenicity-only baseline in the survey, and its documented diculty with gain-of-function variants reinforces the case for direction-aware models.
nAChR structural biology (experimental data). Noviello et al. solved the human 7 receptor
in resting, activated, and desensitised states, and Mazzaferro et al. resolved calcium potentiation of 42 in two stoichiometries. These are not machine-learning methods but supply the statespecic, interface-level structural knowledge our conformational and structural features are designed to capture.
3 How the eld is targeting the problem
The survey reveals a clear split in targets. Most papers target pathogenicity: whether a variant is damaging, measured against ClinVar or deep-mutational-scan labels. A smaller, more recent wave targets direction of eect, motivated by the clinical fact that GOF and LOF demand opposite treatments. This second wave is where our project sits, and the six direction-aware papers are its current state of the art.
Critically, none of the direction-aware tools is built for ligand-gated, pentameric channels specifically. funNCion and the two Boÿelmann et al. methods are limited to voltage-gated channels. LoGoFunc treats every gene uniformly and has no structural context. PreMode is binary GOF/LOF and struggles with disordered regions. MissION aggregates diverse channel families and does not encode subunit identity or pentameric assembly. This is precisely the gap our model targets: a nAChR-specic, subunit-aware, direction-aware predictor.
4 Relevance to our project
The papers map onto our pipeline at four levels.
1. Direct comparators. funNCion, the two Boÿelmann et al. models, LoGoFunc, PreMode,
and MissION dene the baseline we must beat or match. MissION in particular is the closest
2

precursor, and its channel-agnostic design is the limitation we exploit by encoding subunit identity and pentameric context.
2. Feature sources. ESM-2 embeddings and zero-shot scores (Rives, Meier, Lin, Sledzieski),
conservation signals (Frazer, Marquet), structural features from cryo-EM and AlphaFold models (Jumper, Abramson, Noviello, Mazzaferro), clinical phenotype terms (Boÿelmann et al. 2023), and stability estimates (Blaabjerg) are all candidate inputs to our 71-feature representation.
3. Methodological guidance. Brandes et al. show a logistic head on PLM embeddings is a
cheap, eective classier; Sledzieski et al. show LoRA ne-tuning works with very few trainable parameters, which is exactly the setting of a 717-variant dataset; Zhong et al. show a structure GNN transfers with few labels; and Gao et al. show structure-aware convolution outperforms sequence-only models.
4. Evaluation. ProteinGym (Notin et al.) is the template for a shared, versioned benchmark,
which our Future Work proposes for nAChR direction-of-eect prediction.
One cross-cutting observation from the survey is that scaling protein language models has begun to saturate: Chen et al. report only incremental gains at 100B parameters, and our own experiments found ESM-2 650M performs no better than 150M on this task. The more promising levers for direction-of-eect prediction are structure, subunit context, and direction-labelled data, not larger language models.
